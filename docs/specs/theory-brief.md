# Common theory brief (report §formulation, thesis §theory)

This brief fixes the statements and proofs that the report and the thesis proposal must take up consistently. Notation: `docs/specs/notation.md`.

## 0. Setting

- Control parameters $\boldsymbol\lambda \in \Lambda \subset \mathbb{R}^p$ (e.g. $Re$, $Rm$, $\mu_r$, $\Gamma$).
- Discretized dynamics $\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x} = F_{\boldsymbol\lambda}(\mathbf{x})\,\mathrm{d}t + \Sigma\,\mathrm{d}W_t$ ($\Sigma = 0$: deterministic). Generator $\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol\lambda}\varphi = F_{\boldsymbol\lambda}\cdot\nabla\varphi + \tfrac12 \Sigma\Sigma^\top : \nabla^2\varphi$.
- Data: snapshots of a statistically stationary state, with law $\mu_{\boldsymbol\lambda}$ (invariant measure, time average).
- Model: $q_\theta(\cdot\mid\boldsymbol\lambda) = (\Phi^{\boldsymbol\lambda}_1)_\# \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ where $\Phi^{\boldsymbol\lambda}_t$ is the flow of $\dot{\mathbf{z}} = v_\theta(\mathbf{z}, t, \boldsymbol\lambda)$. Flow matching loss on the linear path $\mathbf{x}_t = (1-t)\mathbf{x}_0 + t\mathbf{x}_1$, target $\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_0$; the minimizer is $v^\star(\mathbf{x}, t) = \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_0 \mid \mathbf{x}_t = \mathbf{x}]$.

## 1. Taxonomy of constraints (Definition)

| Class | Form | MHD examples | True for | Suitable mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 local linear | $\mathbf{x} \in S$, $S = \ker A$ closed subspace | $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u} = 0$, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$, homogeneous linear boundary conditions | each sample | architecture (potentials) or projection (e.g. ECI) |
| C2 local nonlinear | $c(\mathbf{x}) = 0$ | stationary state of a PDE (Darcy in PIDM), algebraic constraint | each sample (if no time derivative is involved) | nonlinear projection (PCFM) or penalty/guidance |
| C3 symmetry | $g_\#\mu_{\boldsymbol\lambda} = \mu_{\boldsymbol\lambda}$, $g \in G$ | $\mathbf{B} \to -\mathbf{B}$; rotation $R_\pi$ of VKS; $(Q,D,V)\to(-Q,-D,V)$ | the law | equivariance of the velocity field |
| C4 statistical (stationarity) | $\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\boldsymbol\lambda}}[\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol\lambda}\varphi] = 0$, $\varphi \in \Phi$ | mean energy balance $\langle\varepsilon\rangle = \langle P\rangle$, cross-helicity and $\langle a^2\rangle$ balances | the law only | law-level loss at training time; exponential tilting at sampling time |

Key remark: for a snapshot of a **non-stationary** PDE, the residual $\partial_t\mathbf{x} - F(\mathbf{x})$ cannot be computed (the time derivative is not in the snapshot). Only constraints without a time derivative (divergence, boundary conditions, diagnostic elliptic equations) are per-sample constraints. The energy balance $\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}t = P - \varepsilon$ is an **identity** (the right-hand side is computable), not a constraint; the constraint is $\mathbb{E}[P - \varepsilon] = 0$.

## 2. Statements

**Proposition 1 (C1 by construction).** If the decoder is written $\mathbf{x} = \mathcal{C}(\mathbf{z})$ with $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathcal{C} \subset S$ (e.g. $\mathbf{u} = \nabla^\perp\psi$, $\mathbf{B} = \nabla\times\mathbf{A}$), then $q(S) = 1$ whatever the law of $\mathbf{z}$. In a bounded domain, $\mathcal{C}$ must in addition respect the boundary conditions (e.g. $\psi$ constant on an impermeable wall; gauge and matching to the exterior field for $\mathbf{A}$).

**Proposition 2 (decomposition of the optimal flow on a subspace).** If $\mu(S) = 1$ with $S$ a linear subspace, $P$ the orthogonal projector onto $S$ and $\mathbf{x}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, then for $t < 1$
$$ v^\star(\mathbf{x}, t) = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_1 - P\mathbf{x}_0 \mid P\mathbf{x}_t = P\mathbf{x}]}_{\in S,\ \text{depends only on } P\mathbf{x}} \; - \; \frac{(I-P)\mathbf{x}}{1-t}. $$
*Proof.* $P\mathbf{x}_0$ and $(I-P)\mathbf{x}_0$ are independent Gaussians (isotropy). $\mathbf{x}_1 \in S$ so $(I-P)\mathbf{x}_t = (1-t)(I-P)\mathbf{x}_0$, independent of $(P\mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{x}_1, P\mathbf{x}_0)$. We condition each component. ∎
*Consequences.* (a) With exact $v^\star$, free sampling lands exactly in $S$: $(I-P)\mathbf{x}(t) = (1-t)(I-P)\mathbf{x}(0)$. (b) In the limit of perfect learning, free sampling, final projection, projected flow and potentials give **the same law**: the observed differences come from approximation error and inductive bias, not from the target. (c) *A missing contraction leaves a divergence of order one.* If, along a trajectory, $(I-P)v_\theta = -\kappa(t)(I-P)\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{r}(t)$ with $0 \le \kappa \le K$ and $\|\mathbf{r}\| \le \rho$, then $\|(I-P)\mathbf{x}(1)\| \ge e^{-K}\|(I-P)\mathbf{x}(0)\| - \rho$. By contrast, a bounded error **added** to the exact field is erased: $(I-P)\mathbf{x}(t) = (1-t)\big[(I-P)\mathbf{x}(0) + \int_0^t \frac{(I-P)\mathbf{r}(s)}{1-s}\mathrm{d}s\big] \to 0$, since $(1-t)\ln\frac{1}{1-t} \to 0$. (d) *Discrete sampling.* With the exact field, the orthogonal equation $\dot{\mathbf{y}} = -\mathbf{y}/(1-t)$ has the affine solution $(1-t)\mathbf{y}(0)$, which Euler on a grid aligned with $t = 1$ integrates **exactly** ($\mathbf{y}_{k+1} = \mathbf{y}_k (1-t_{k+1})/(1-t_k)$). Heun's corrector evaluates the field at $t = 1$, where the exact field is singular ($0/0$), so Heun needs a stop before $t = 1$ or a network whose field is finite there. With a bounded error $\mathbf{r}$, the last Euler step leaves the residual $h\,\mathbf{r}(t_{N-1})$; a grid that is not aligned with $t = 1$ (a step crossing $t = 1$) is unstable. *Reading of E2 (hypothesis, not measured):* the relative divergence 0.41 of the free model V0 is a representation error of the network in the sense of (c), not an integration error. *(Corrections of 7 and 11 November 2026: an earlier version stated that the final orthogonal component was $(I-P)e(1)$, then that an explicit scheme resolves the stiff term poorly; both were wrong, see review B1.)*

**Proposition 3 (unbiased penalty for a linear constraint; Jensen gap otherwise).** The end-of-path prediction $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1 = \mathbf{x}_t + (1-t)v(\mathbf{x}_t, t)$ equals $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_1 \mid \mathbf{x}_t]$ at $v = v^\star$. For $A$ linear with $A\mathbf{x}_1 = 0$ a.s., the penalty $\|A\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1\|^2$ vanishes at $v^\star$: it does not move the optimum. For nonlinear $c$, $c(\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_1\mid\mathbf{x}_t]) \neq \mathbb{E}[c(\mathbf{x}_1)\mid\mathbf{x}_t] = 0$ in general: the penalty biases the optimum (Jensen gap discussed by Bastek, Sun, Kochmann 2025).
*Proof.* $\mathbf{x}_t + (1-t)(\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_0) = \mathbf{x}_1$, then linearity of conditional expectation. ∎

**Proposition 4 (exact symmetry).** Let $G$ be a finite group acting by linear isometries. If $v(g\mathbf{x}, t, \boldsymbol\lambda) = g\,v(\mathbf{x}, t, \boldsymbol\lambda)$ for all $g$ and the base law is $G$-invariant, then $q(\cdot\mid\boldsymbol\lambda)$ is $G$-invariant. The symmetrization $v_{\mathrm{sym}} = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_g g^{-1}v(g\,\cdot)$ is equivariant, and if $\mu$ is $G$-invariant, the (population) flow matching loss satisfies $\mathcal{J}(v_{\mathrm{sym}}) \le \mathcal{J}(v)$.
*Proof.* Uniqueness of ODE solutions: $g\Phi_t(\mathbf{z})$ and $\Phi_t(g\mathbf{z})$ solve the same ODE with the same initial datum. Image measure: $g_\#q = g_\#(\Phi_1)_\#\gamma = (\Phi_1)_\# g_\#\gamma = q$. For the loss: $\mathcal{J}$ is convex in $v$ and invariant under $v \mapsto g^{-1}v(g\,\cdot)$ when $\mu$ and $\gamma$ are invariant; Jensen. ∎
*Consequence (E1).* The stationarity identities (C4) do not fix the relative weights of two symmetric ergodic components: the set of invariant measures is convex, and the ergodic measures are its extreme points. Below the reversal threshold, any combination $p\,\mu_+ + (1-p)\,\mu_-$ is invariant. The symmetry must be imposed separately (Proposition 4).

**Proposition 5 (stationarity identities).** If $\mu$ is invariant, then $\mathbb{E}_\mu[\mathcal{L}\varphi] = 0$ for every smooth $\varphi$ with controlled growth (weak form of the stationary Liouville / Fokker–Planck equation $\nabla\cdot(\mu F) = \tfrac12\nabla^2 : (\Sigma\Sigma^\top\mu)$). The mean energy balance is the case $\varphi = E$.
*Proof (deterministic).* For $\mathbf{x}_0 \sim \mu$, $\varphi(\mathbf{x}_t) - \varphi(\mathbf{x}_0) = \int_0^t \mathcal{L}\varphi(\mathbf{x}_s)\mathrm{d}s$; expectation, invariance ($\mathbf{x}_s \sim \mu$), Fubini. With noise: Itô formula, the stochastic integral has zero expectation. ∎

**Corollary 6 (inconsistent per-sample constraint).** If the law of $\mathcal{L}\varphi(\mathbf{x})$ under $\mu$ puts **no mass at 0** ($\mu(\{\mathcal{L}\varphi = 0\}) = 0$), any law concentrated on $\{\mathcal{L}\varphi = 0\}$ is singular with respect to $\mu$ ($\mathrm{KL} = \infty$). If moreover $\mathcal{L}\varphi$ is $L$-Lipschitz on a domain carrying the laws considered, every such law $\nu$ satisfies $W_1(\nu, \mu) \ge \mathbb{E}_\mu|\mathcal{L}\varphi|/L > 0$: the error of the per-sample projection does not vanish, even with a perfect generator.
*Proof.* Singularity by definition; for the bound, $\mathbb{E}|\mathcal{L}\varphi(\mathbf{Y})| = \mathbb{E}|\mathcal{L}\varphi(\mathbf{Y}) - \mathcal{L}\varphi(\mathbf{X})| \le L\,\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{X} - \mathbf{Y}\|$ for any coupling of $\nu$ and $\mu$. ∎
*The assumption is not implied by $\mathrm{Var}_\mu(\mathcal{L}\varphi) > 0$.* Counterexample: in a bistable regime, a mixture of a fluctuating state and a fixed point ("off" state, where $\mathcal{L}\varphi = 0$ identically) has $\mathrm{Var} > 0$ and $\mu(\{\mathcal{L}\varphi = 0\}) > 0$. The assumption must be checked on the reference (E1: $\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}t$ has a continuous histogram; E2: the dissipation $\mathcal{D}$ fluctuates continuously). Measured: E1 (variance of $\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}t$ brought down to 0, degraded energy distance) and E2 (per-sample rescaling of the small scales).

**Proposition 7 (I-projection).** Assume $Z(\boldsymbol\beta) = \mathbb{E}_q[e^{\boldsymbol\beta\cdot g}] < \infty$ and $c$ in the interior of the convex hull of the values of $g$ under $q$ (otherwise the minimum of the dual is not attained). Among the laws $\nu \ll q$ such that $\mathbb{E}_\nu[g] = c$, the one closest to $q$ in the sense of $\mathrm{KL}(\nu\,\|\,q)$ is $\nu^\star = e^{\boldsymbol\beta\cdot g}q/Z(\boldsymbol\beta)$, with $\boldsymbol\beta$ minimizing the convex dual $\log Z(\boldsymbol\beta) - \boldsymbol\beta\cdot c$ (Csiszár 1975; proof by the Pythagorean identity $\mathrm{KL}(\nu\|q) = \mathrm{KL}(\nu\|\nu^\star) + \mathrm{KL}(\nu^\star\|q)$, report §3). The tilting is exact **only for the imposed moments**, and only on the tuning sample; it is useful where the ESS holds. On a sample: self-normalized weights, ESS $= 1/\sum w_i^2$. Limit: reweighting does not create mass; if $q$ misses a region that $\mu$ charges (e.g. the "bridge" $|D| \approx 0$ near the reversal threshold), the ESS collapses. Hence the **stationarity loss at training time**, $\beta\sum_k(\mathbb{E}_{q_\theta(\cdot\mid\boldsymbol\lambda)}[\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol\lambda}\varphi_k]/s_k)^2$ evaluated by differentiable sampling, which also applies at $\boldsymbol\lambda$ **without data**.

**Proposition 8 (Rice formula: frequency of zero crossings of $D$ from the law).** Let $D(t) = \ell(\mathbf{x}(t))$ be a linear observable (dipole moment) of a stationary process with law $\mu$ and $C^1$ trajectories, such that the joint law of $(D, \dot D)$ has a continuous density $p(u, \dot u)$. The mean number of zero crossings of $D$ per unit time is
$$ \nu_0 = \int |\dot u|\,p(0, \dot u)\,\mathrm{d}\dot u = \lim_{h\to0}\frac{1}{2h}\mathbb{E}_\mu\big[|\ell(F(\mathbf{x}))|\,\mathbf{1}_{|\ell(\mathbf{x})|<h}\big] $$
(Kac 1943; Rice 1944, 1945; proof: counting formula on a transverse $C^1$ trajectory, then expectation, report §3). It depends only on the stationary law and on $F$: a snapshot generator predicts the **frequency of zero crossings of $D$ (reversals and excursions)** without integrating in time. In MHD, $\dot D = \ell(\nabla\times(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{B}) + \eta\Delta\mathbf{B})$ is computable on a generated snapshot $(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{B})$. Limits: $F$ is needed; every zero crossing is counted, not only clear-cut reversals; the law of waiting times is not given; for a deterministic chaotic dynamics (E1) the density assumption is not guaranteed and one works at finite window $h$. Windowed estimator: bias $O(h^2)$ if $f(u) = \int|\dot u|p(u,\dot u)\mathrm{d}\dot u$ is $C^2$, variance $O(1/(Nh))$.

**Proposition 9 (error bound near a jump in the law).** If $\mathbf{z}\mapsto v_\theta$ is $L_x$-Lipschitz and $\boldsymbol\lambda\mapsto v_\theta$ is $L_\lambda$-Lipschitz, then (Grönwall) $W_1(q_{\boldsymbol\lambda}, q_{\boldsymbol\lambda'}) \le L_\lambda\frac{e^{L_x}-1}{L_x}|\boldsymbol\lambda - \boldsymbol\lambda'| =: K|\boldsymbol\lambda - \boldsymbol\lambda'|$. If the true family jumps at $\lambda_c$, in the sense that $J_\delta = W_1(\mu_{\lambda_c-\delta}, \mu_{\lambda_c+\delta}) \to J > 0$ as $\delta \to 0$ (subcritical bifurcation, multistability), then for every $\delta > 0$, $\max_{\lambda \in \{\lambda_c\pm\delta\}} W_1(q_\lambda, \mu_\lambda) \ge (J_\delta - 2K\delta)/2$. A generator that is regular in $\boldsymbol\lambda$ makes an error of at least $J/2$ near the jump, unless it has a large constant $K$ (or conditioning on the state/hysteresis).
*Proof.* Triangle inequality between $\mu_{\lambda_1}, q_{\lambda_1}, q_{\lambda_2}, \mu_{\lambda_2}$ with $\lambda_{1,2} = \lambda_c \mp \delta$. ∎ For a supercritical bifurcation (dynamo with continuous threshold, amplitude $\propto\sqrt{Rm - Rm_c}$), the law varies continuously but as $\sqrt{\cdot}$: infinite slope at the threshold, the same difficulty in attenuated form. *E1 does not test this proposition as stated:* the **symmetrized** law that the generator learns varies continuously at the reversal threshold (bridge mass $\mathbb{P}(|D|<0.25)$ born from 0 with a steep slope); only the law of a single trajectory jumps. E1 shows a **steep rise**, and $J_\delta$ computed on the reference is reported in the digest.

## 3. Experimental plan of the internship (carried out in `experiments/`, CPU scale)

- **E1** (Gissinger 2012, 3 modes): Propositions 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8; Proposition 9 only through the **steep rise** of the bridge mass and of the crossing frequency at the threshold (the symmetrized law varies continuously; $J_\delta$ on the reference is reported in the digest). Threshold of zero crossings of $D$ **observed between $\Gamma = 0.86$ and $0.865$** on the fine grid (measured in this repository, unpublished), interpreted as an attractor-merging crisis **by analogy** with the $\mu$ scenario of Gissinger (2012); the nature of the transition is not established here. "No crossing for $\Gamma \le 0.86$" means none observed over the simulated time.
- **E2** (forced 2D MHD, truncated Fourier basis: a POD basis, without reduction, up to a rotation; the Fourier modes are the POD modes of a translation-invariant law, and they rotate once $H_C \neq 0$ couples $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{B}$): Propositions 1, 2, 3 (four constraint mechanisms against the free baseline V0), 5, 6, 7 (energy balance, cross-helicity and $\langle a^2\rangle$ at the law level).
- **E3** (ABC kinematic dynamo): validation of the 3D stage. The growth rate changes sign three times in $2 \le Rm \le 30$ (≈ 8.9, 17.5 and 26–27 depending on the fit window; digest), the dynamo windows reported by Galloway et al. as quoted in Archontis et al. (2003); Bouya & Dormy (2013) place a mode crossing near $Rm \approx 24$. Divergence at machine precision through the rotational form.

**What this work claims (worded cautiously: "to our knowledge, after a literature search on 6 November 2026"):**
1. Using the stationarity identities of a **field PDE** (MHD: energy, cross-helicity and $\langle a^2\rangle$ balances) as **law-level** constraints of a **parametric conditional** generative model, enforceable at parameters **without data** (E1 fine-tuning, E2 law-level loss), and **identities with no target value** (unlike the external moments of Khalifa or Mun).
2. Stating and **measuring** the inconsistency of the per-sample constraint of a stationary balance (Corollary 6): E1 and E2.
3. **Rice formula applied to a snapshot generator** to predict the frequency of zero crossings of $D$ (reversals and excursions) from the generated law and $F$, without time integration (Proposition 8): no prior work found.
4. Symmetry / stationarity complementarity: the identities do not fix the weights of the ergodic components; equivariance is necessary (E1).
5. Proposition 9 (lower bound on the error near a jump in the law) applied to the dynamo threshold.

**Not claimed (classical technique):** the unbiased estimator of $(\mathbb{E}g)^2$ by the product of the means of two independent half-batches (double sampling, U-statistic); it is used in E1 and E2, and the naive estimator (mini-batch mean)$^2 = (\mathbb{E}g)^2 + \mathrm{Var}(g)/n$ penalizes the dispersion of the law.

**Points of caution:** the identities are necessary but not sufficient (every invariant measure satisfies them, including those carried by unstable orbits): keep the tie to the data; the selection of the physical measure by the zero-noise limit is guaranteed only in the Axiom A setting (Young 2002); the SRB measure is in general singular, hence the weak form.
