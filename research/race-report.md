## Measured

- Zero divergence is exact by design (vector potentials) or by projection (1e-8); a divergence penalty leaves 0.26 and doubles the distance to the reference law.
- Every model of the first protocol is 117 to 519 noise floors from the reference in energy distance. The same network with an explicit mode-wise affine term in the velocity field (W9) is at 9.3 floors (factor 22 against its matched control at 205).
- In energy distance, no learned model beats two Gaussian laws without a network. Per Fourier mode (G0): 3.5 floors, but no magnetic production (magnetic balance residual 0.998). With the covariance between modes (G1, fitted on the training η): 2.5 floors, magnetic balance 0.072, null-count error 1.10 against 1.66 for W9; confirmed on a second realisation of the data. W9 has a lower residual of the magnetic balance on the mean over η (0.055), which is not an advantage: it comes from two errors that cancel (next item).
- The reference law is non-Gaussian in its magnetic production. Split exactly into a mean-flow part (second-order statistics) and a third-order part, the production of the reference has a third-order part of +0.054, −0.137 and −0.270 of the Ohmic dissipation at the three held-out η (+0.14 to −0.31 over the eleven η). The Gaussian laws have none (below 0.002). W9 has −0.006, −0.082 and −0.118: 0.60 and 0.44 of the reference at the two lower η, in every seed and on both data realisations (H25, which predicted none, is refuted), and a small value of the opposite sign at the largest. Its mean-flow part is 10 to 16 % low at the held-out η: the magnetic balance of W9 (production over dissipation 0.79, 0.93, 1.02) is the sum of a mean-flow error of −0.15, −0.13, −0.13 and a third-order error of −0.06, +0.06, +0.15, which cancel at the two lower η and add at the largest.
- The restart drift of objective T1 (relative to each ensemble's own initial energy, at τ = 4) does not rank the laws. Anchored on the reference energy and followed to τ = 16, the six laws tested that start at or below the reference magnetic energy (G0, G1, G2, G3, W9, W11) gain 10 to 21 % of it after the restart (W10, which starts 21 % above, relaxes downwards; that the rises have one cause is not established): W9 starts 12 % low and comes back to the reference (overshoot 0.0045, 0.7 % off at τ = 16); G1 and G0 start on it and overshoot it by 9 % and 17 % before coming back towards it (H26 confirmed on its registered criterion, both realisations; it does not show that W9 is closer to the attractor than G1). The largest distance to the reference energy over the course is comparable for W9 and G1 (0.13 and 0.10).
- On the measure registered for H27 (the largest deviation from the reference energy beyond the initial one), the Gaussian law with the mean and covariance of 8192 W9 samples (G2) does not move farther than W9 (H27 refuted on the second data realisation; on the first the null control of the test missed its threshold by 0.004 and the test is invalid as written). That measure cannot see a passage above the reference smaller than the 12 % initial deficit. G2 loses the third-order production, has a null-count error higher by 0.04 ± 0.03 out of 1.7, is 0.43 floors from W9 in energy distance (about twice the sampling value) and 0.6 to 0.9 floors farther from the reference; its drift at τ = 4 is 0.170 against 0.124 for W9 (paired +0.045 ± 0.007, both realisations). Of the metrics of the first protocol, the energy distance and the null count hardly see the non-Gaussian part of W9; the restart drift does.
- G1, the Gaussian law with the moments of the reference, has 2.1 times its null points on the mean over η, at the training η as at the held-out ones (2.5 times at the largest η, 1.6 at the smallest); at the held-out η, the only ones where the restart was run and where G1 is interpolated, it leaves the stationary energy by 9 to 11 %.
- A law-level stationarity loss lowers the residual of the energy identity at η without data (0.29 → 0.19 on a wrong model, 0.057 → 0.029 on a nearly right one), not that of the identity E[⟨j·B⟩] = 0, and on the nearly right model it raises the distance from 9.3 to 16.4 floors. Exponential tilting on the same identities collapses on the wrong model and improves the nearly right one on most metrics (energy residual 0.011, effective sample 17 %).
- Longer training (8×) and wider networks do not close the gap: the loss falls and the law gets worse (W9: 9.3 → 18.8 floors; plain network: 205 floors at width 512, 25.6 at width 2048, 104 at width 5632).
- No law within 60 floors of the reference has the reference number of magnetic null points (2.1 to 3.5 times), and none is stationary under the solver.
- An independent replication on other data seeds changes no verdict of H4–H17, H19, H20, H22, H25 and H26; H27 is decided on the second realisation only; H18, H21, H23 and H24 rest on one realisation of the data. Hypotheses of E4 (H4–H27): 10 confirmed, 13 refuted, 1 dropped; every verdict given by `encadrant` on five seeds, all reviews in `research/reviews/` and `research/reviews.jsonl`. Two interpretations of the main result were refuted by `jury-externe` and withdrawn: "third-order correlations" as the reason why the learned models beat G0 (v1), and the ranking of the laws by the restart drift (v2, v3).

## Conjectured

That the third-order production held by W9 is part of what keeps it from passing above the reference energy at restart, as its Gaussianisation does by 2.5 % more (computed after the fact on both realisations, not registered; the difference is largest, 0.039 ± 0.008, at the one held-out η where the two have the same production, so it is not the whole cause); that a model with the covariance of G1 and the third-order production of the reference would be closer to stationary under the solver (which statistic of the magnetic field sets its number of null points is not known); that the failure of plain networks at large width or long training is memorisation of the 14 400 training snapshots; that the ranking of the mechanisms carries over to turbulent regimes. Why every law that starts at or below the reference energy gains magnetic energy at restart is not explained. Literature searches found no generative model of dynamo simulations and no use of the helicity identity, null counts or a restart test for generative models: negative statements, not proofs.
