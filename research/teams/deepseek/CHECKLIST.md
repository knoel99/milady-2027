# NeurIPS paper checklist (answers)

**1. Claims.** The claims are the measured comparisons in `results/metrics.json` and the tables in
`REPORT.md`: divergence control by architectural parameterisation, the effect of a physics-informed
loss on energy balance/PDE residual, and parameter-conditioning behaviour on held-out and
interpolation parameter grids. No claim of state-of-the-art performance or of converged 3D MHD is
made. `REPORT.md` §6 states the limits.

**2. Limitations.** `REPORT.md` §6: 2D, 64² grid, 32-component latent, 256 training fields per
benchmark, Re,Rm ≤ 50, decaying (not forced) turbulence, weak linear parameter signal in field
space, 2D helicity identity. The pilot is not a substitute for the 5–6 month internship.

**3. Theory.** No new theoretical result is claimed. The method composes published flow matching
(Lipman et al. 2023; Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden 2023) with exact spectral divergence-free
parameterisation and a discrete energy/PDE residual; the derivation of the 2D MHD vorticity–flux
equations used by the solver is standard and is repeated in `REPORT.md` §3.1.

**4. Experiments.** Code, data and trained weights are included; every reported number is
regenerated by `reproduce.sh`. Three training seeds per variant (two for the parameter-blind
ablation) with seed-level means; held-out test and interpolation parameter grids; reference floors
for energy balance and PDE residual are reported next to model values.

**5. Reproducibility.** Datasets are generated deterministically from explicit seeds
(`code/make_data.py`); training seeds are explicit; `reproduce.sh` runs data → training → metrics →
figures; `requirements.txt` pins versions. CPU-only, no external services needed except Python
packages.

**6. Statistical significance.** Wilcoxon signed-rank tests over parameter pairs (n=16
in-distribution, n=9 interpolation) on seed-averaged per-pair metrics are reported in
`results/sanity_checks.json` (`code/check_results.py`). With three seeds and 8–24 fields per
parameter pair, the tests are indicative, not definitive; this is stated in the report.

**7. Compute.** 4 vCPU, 15 GB RAM, no GPU. Data generation ≈ 6 min, training ≈ 10 min, evaluation
≈ 5 min (see `COSTS.md` for the framework's ledger and the report for the breakdown). No cloud
resources were created.

**8. Ethics.** No human subjects, personal data or sensitive attributes. The work is a physics
benchmark; the main misuse risk (generating plausible-looking but unphysical fields) is mitigated by
reporting divergence, energy-balance and PDE residuals alongside distributional metrics.

**9. Broader impacts.** Positive: reusable open benchmark and code for physics-constrained
generative modelling of MHD; supports data assimilation and reduced-order modelling. Negative
impacts are not specific to this work.

**10. Safeguards.** Trained weights are small benchmark models; no dual-use capability beyond
standard scientific generative modelling. Released under MIT (code) and CC-BY-4.0 (data).

**11. Licenses of assets.** All code and data are original, produced by this mission. No third-party
dataset or model weights are redistributed. The cited papers are referenced, not copied. Repository
licence: MIT; data licence: CC-BY-4.0.

**12. Use of AI.** All scientific work — solver, models, experiments, figures, report — was produced
by the DeepSeek Harness agent (`deepseek-v4-pro`, reasoning `high`) with the mission's tools. The
statement is repeated in `REPORT.md` §8.
