# Race brief

This file is the prompt of every team. It is identical for all teams and owned by the user; agents never edit it.

## Goal

Advance the research of the MilaDy 2027 internship as far as possible in one week, within your budget. The internship offer (`docs/specs/sources/supervisors.md`, §1.2 a) asks for physics-constrained generative models (diffusion or flow matching) of parametric dynamical systems: 3D fields conditioned on parameters such as $Re$ and $Rm$, constraints imposed "through model design, training losses, or sampling procedures" (energy balance, divergence-free conditions), evaluated with "energy spectra, helicity, divergence control, and topology preservation", leading to a comparative study and code integrated into the project toolbox.

You start from this repository at the tag `race-start`. Everything in it (theory brief, experiments E1–E3, report, thesis proposal) was written before the race and **is not validated**: refuting a proposition, a result or a claim of the report earns as much as producing a new result, provided the refutation follows the rules below.

## Your setting

- You work on your own branch `race/<team>` on your own machine. You cannot see the other teams.
- `MILADY_PROVIDER` names your provider's section of `agents/providers.toml`. Call the roles (`encadrant`, `physicien`, `biblio`, `jury-externe`) only through `tools/call_model.py` or `tools/run_role.py`, as `AGENTS.md` says; every call is logged and audited.
- **Budget.** Your provider API key is capped by the user in your provider's console (see `race/config.toml`); the cap is final and also covers role calls. Computations larger than your machine go through the compute broker (`tools/compute_broker.py submit`), which enforces your CPU quota and returns cached results when the exact same run was already computed.
- **Time.** The race ends at the deadline in `race/config.toml`. Commit your work at least once per hour: only committed work is collected and judged.

## Day 1: quantify your objectives

Before any computation, write `race/teams/<team>/objectives.toml` from `race/objectives.template.toml`:

1. Quantify every common objective of `race/objectives.common.toml`: your target, and the command that measures it.
2. Add at most five objectives of your own, each tied to a sentence of the offer.
3. Measure each objective on the starting repository (`baseline`), with its command.

Commit the file within the first 24 hours, then carry on. The judge locks the version committed at that point (`tools/race_progress.py lock`) and the user may reject an objective that does not measure what it claims; after the lock, any change to the file voids your progress score. The progress of each objective is measured every day by the judge, by running your command on your branch from a clean checkout.

## Rules

1. `AGENTS.md` applies in full: register every hypothesis in `research/hypotheses.json` with its quantitative refutation criterion before the computation, `--quick` first, at least five seeds for any quoted comparison, numbers only from `experiments/results/digest.md` or files generated by committed scripts.
2. Do not re-run the existing E1–E3 full runs: their results are in `experiments/results/`. Re-running them costs your budget and earns nothing.
3. A measurement command reads committed result files and prints, on its last line, a JSON object `{"value": <number>}` (optionally `"seeds": <n>`). It must finish in less than 10 minutes on 4 CPU cores and must not train a model.
4. Write a journal entry in `research/journal.md` at least once per day: what you tried, what failed, what you will do next.
5. Do not open the published site of the project (knoel99.github.io) nor the reports, results or branches of the other teams.
6. Do not modify `race/`, `tools/race_*.py`, `tools/check_calls.py`, `tools/call_model.py`, `tools/run_role.py`, `tools/compute_broker.py` or `agents/`: the judge runs its own copies from `main`.
7. When your budget is spent or the deadline is reached, write `research/race-report.md` (one page: what is measured, what is published, what is conjectured, your remaining work), commit and stop.

## How you are judged

1. **Gates** (pass or fail): `tools/check_gates.py`, `make test`, `python3 tools/check_calls.py ledger`, every number traced to the digest or a committed result file, no `[UNCONFIRMED]` source used.
2. **Reproduction**: the judge re-runs your key computations; a result that does not reproduce is removed and penalised.
3. **Progress**: per objective, `(value − baseline) / (target − baseline)`, clipped to [0, 1]; the remaining work is reported for each objective not reached. Common objectives are also compared across teams on their raw values.
4. **Blind review**: `jury-externe`, run on another provider than yours on an anonymised copy of your branch, gives a verdict per claim. The user arbitrates the final ranking.
5. **Time and cost**: the time to the final delivery (first commit of the team to the commit of `research/GOAL-MET`) and the total cost (LLM calls at API-equivalent prices plus GPU time and the team's share of the VM) are compared across teams. Scientific quality comes first: time and cost separate teams of comparable quality, and a result bought with fewer seeds, no registered criterion or no review is penalised under 1 and 4.
