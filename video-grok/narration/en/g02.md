# g02 — Stretch against smear

## g02.stretch

duration: 1:00
badge: CONTEXT

### cue.g02.stretch.show

role: spoken

Moving fluid stretches a magnetic field. Picture one field line in that flow. Two neighboring parcels move apart along the line, so the line gets longer. The same motion squeezes the line sideways, so the line gets thinner. Where the line is longer and thinner, the field is stronger. Diffusion works against that thinning. It spreads the thin line's field into the neighboring fluid, and the sharp part fades. Ohmic dissipation is that fade. The fluid turns magnetic energy into heat. Stretch and diffusion run together. When the flow pulls faster than diffusion smooths, the field can grow. When diffusion wins, the field dies. Diffusion is stronger where the field changes across a short distance. A thin line is that place. The fluid carries the field, and the same fluid lets it leak.

### cue.g02.ratio.formula

### cue.g02.ratio.show

role: spoken

Induction is the stretch of the field by the flow. Diffusion is the smear. The magnetic Reynolds number compares induction with diffusion. It is a length, times a speed, divided by the magnetic diffusivity. Magnetic diffusivity is how fast diffusion smears the field. Induction becomes stronger relative to diffusion. A higher speed raises the ratio. The rise does not by itself mean the threshold has been crossed. Rm is not a measurement until both are named.

## g02.threshold

duration: 0:40
badge: CONTEXT

### cue.g02.threshold.show

role: spoken

Under the threshold, diffusion wins and the field dies. Above the threshold, the field can grow. The threshold is a place on that ratio, not a curve taken from one named flow. No ABC dynamo is drawn in this chapter. No number is attached to the crossing yet. Growth is allowed above the threshold. It is not promised for every fluid, and it is not promised at every boundary. The picture is only the idea of a threshold. The sodium that follows is the fluid that makes the threshold hard to reach, because the flow is already wild.

## g02.sodium

duration: 0:30
badge: CONTEXT

### cue.g02.sodium.show

role: spoken

Sodium carries a very small magnetic Prandtl number. At 120 degrees Celsius the value is 0.82 times 10 to the minus 5. Divide the magnetic Reynolds number by that Prandtl number and the kinetic Reynolds number stands far above it. The fluid is turbulent well before a dynamo can appear. The field does not grow in a quiet flow. Turbulence is the setting. It is not a correction added later.

### cue.g02.close.show

Stretch fights diffusion. The ratio says which one wins. Above the threshold the field can grow. Sodium is turbulent before that growth.
