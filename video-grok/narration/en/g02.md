# g02 — Stretch against smear

## g02.stretch

duration: 1:00
badge: CONTEXT

### cue.g02.stretch.show

role: spoken

Moving fluid stretches a magnetic field. Diffusion smears that field back toward nothing. Look at the picture of those two actions before any formula. A line is drawn out by the flow, and ohmic dissipation eats it back. The image comes first so the ratio has something to name. A historical disk dynamo has no pinned source here. No disk threshold is stated. The time that picture would have used stays on this stretch, and it adds no new idea. The comparison of stretch and smear is the whole of the scene. Later rooms of liquid sodium will show how hard the threshold is to reach. They do not change this picture. Hold the stretch. Hold the smear.

### cue.g02.ratio.show

role: spoken

The magnetic Reynolds number compares induction with diffusion. Take a length, multiply by a speed, and divide by the magnetic diffusivity. The names on the glass light one at a time. The voice does not read the symbols. The same ratio changes meaning when the length changes, and it changes meaning when the speed changes. Every later axis has to say which length and which speed it used. A bare number called Rm is not yet a measurement. Write the definition in the picture, or do not write the number.

## g02.threshold

duration: 0:40
badge: CONTEXT

### cue.g02.threshold.show

role: spoken

Under the threshold, diffusion wins and the field dies. Above the threshold, the field can grow. The threshold is a place on that ratio, not a curve taken from one named flow. No ABC dynamo is drawn in this chapter. No number is attached to the crossing yet. Growth is allowed above the threshold. It is not promised for every fluid, and it is not promised at every boundary. The picture is only the idea of a threshold. The sodium that follows is the fluid that makes the threshold hard to reach, because the flow is already wild.

## g02.sodium

duration: 0:30
badge: CONTEXT

### cue.g02.sodium.show

role: spoken

Sodium carries a very small magnetic Prandtl number. At 120 degrees Celsius the value is 0.82 times 10 to the minus 5. Divide the magnetic Reynolds number by that Prandtl number and the kinetic Reynolds number is several million. The fluid is turbulent well before a dynamo can appear. The field does not grow in a quiet flow. Turbulence is the setting. It is not a correction added later.
