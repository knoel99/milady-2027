# g03 — Three rooms of sodium

## g03.riga

duration: 0:40
badge: CONTEXT

### cue.g03.riga.show

role: spoken

Riga was the first liquid-sodium experiment to show a self-excited magnetic mode. In November 1999, at 2150 revolutions per minute and 205 degrees Celsius, that mode grows at 0.0315 per second, plus or minus 0.0009, and it oscillates at 1.326 hertz. The PRL cites two motors of 55 kilowatts. The growing mode is the self-excited one. Vessel dimensions stay off this picture.

### cue.g03.riga.caveat

role: spoken

Other motor ratings describe a later machine. They stay off this picture too.

## g03.karlsruhe

duration: 0:30
badge: CONTEXT

### cue.g03.karlsruhe.show

role: spoken

Karlsruhe ran a successful dynamo in December 1999, a few weeks after Riga. The module holds 52 vortex generators. The saturated field is a stationary dipole, and it has two nearly equivalent states. The dipole can sit in either state. The full text of the earliest short paper was not read, so this picture adds no further number. Do not borrow a Reynolds number from another room.

## g03.vks

duration: 0:55
badge: CONTEXT

### cue.g03.vks.show

role: spoken

VKS in Cadarache stirs liquid sodium between two counter-rotating impellers. Stainless steel impellers gave no dynamo. Soft iron impellers did. Soft iron is what let this experiment reach a dynamo. With an impeller factor of about 0.6, the critical magnetic Reynolds number is about 31. That factor belongs with the 31. Reversals appear when the impellers turn at 16 hertz and 22 hertz. The two polarities last 120 seconds and 50 seconds. One reversal lasts about 5 seconds. Ohmic diffusion takes about 0.4 seconds.

### cue.g03.vks.caveat

role: spoken

The drawing of this device on the site is schematic. Its durations and its amplitudes are not the VKS measurements. Read the numbers from the papers, not from the curves on that drawing. The schematic curves stay out of the measurement.

## g03.dresdyn

duration: 0:20
badge: CONTEXT

### cue.g03.dresdyn.show

role: spoken

DRESDYN is a precessing cylinder at HZDR in Dresden. It is objective two, a search over the ratio of precession to rotation. The pages this film may cite give no numeric window. None is invented. Simplified boundaries are said to underestimate the threshold, with no figure. A dynamo there is not claimed.
