role: unread

### cue.g07.odes.show

role: spoken

mu is the coefficient on Q. nu damps the dipole. Gamma is the forcing. The paper holds Gamma at 0.9, sets nu to 0.1, and sweeps mu. The crisis sits near mu 0.11928. E1 holds mu at 0.119 and sweeps Gamma instead. Those two paths cut the same model in different directions.

## g07.threshold

### cue.g07.threshold.show

role: spoken

The dipole D does not cross zero for any Gamma up to 0.86. At Gamma 0.865 the crossing rate is 0.00194 per unit time. Below that step the dipole stays on one side. Above it, zero crossings begin. The change sits between two Gamma values the training already saw. Both neighbors are already on the training grid, so the threshold is a bracket. There is no sharper point in the data than those two values.

### cue.g07.inherit.show

If you expected a symmetric cloud, this one is not. Read that gap against the floor. Do not read it against zero. The floor is the line that says whether the lean is larger than sampling noise.

## g07.equiv

duration: 0:40
badge: MEASURED

### cue.g07.equiv.show

role: spoken

A symmetric augmentation holds the asymmetry only at Gamma values the training saw. Between those values it slips. Seen values and unseen values are not the same test. Augmentation can memorize the points it was shown. Equivariance has to hold in the gaps, and that is the harder claim. Equivariance holds the asymmetry at the floor 0.0040 everywhere in this sweep. The energy distances fall from 0.0118 to 0.00356, then to 0.00257. The energy floor beside those three distances is 0.00123. The asymmetry floor and the energy floor are different lines. Do not swap them.
