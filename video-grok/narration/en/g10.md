# g10 — Where the constraint is true

## g10.classes

duration: 0:45
badge: PROVED

### cue.g10.classes.show

role: spoken

A constraint can sit in four places. The first two places act on each field, as a local repair or a penalty. The last two act on the law, as a symmetry or a budget identity. A symmetry has to survive every parameter, even one you never trained on. A budget talks about an average, not about one frame. A short bar is not a prize. The place of the constraint is the claim.

## g10.sym

duration: 0:30
badge: PROVED

### cue.g10.sym.show

role: spoken

Changing the sign of the magnetic field B is a symmetry of the law. Equivariance holds that symmetry at every parameter, including parameters with no data. Budget identities do not set the weights of the two polarities. A budget can balance and still put the wrong weight on each side. The identities can be true and still leave the weights free.

## g10.bias

duration: 0:25
badge: PROVED

### cue.g10.bias.show

role: spoken

The square of a mean penalizes variance. An average that you then square punishes a cloud for being wide, even when the true mean is already right. Split the samples into two half-batches and that push disappears. The two halves multiply, so the penalty on width drops out. The identity can clean a loss. It does not by itself pick a model.
