# g09 — Drawing a field from noise

## g09.path

duration: 0:40
badge: PROVED

### cue.g09.path.show

role: spoken

Start from Gaussian noise. Draw segments that point toward the data. Each segment is a step from noise toward a snapshot. Average the directions and you get a field of arrows. The velocity of the generative field is that average. The drawing is a toy. It is not a measurement. The arrows are not a velocity from a sodium tank, and they are not a magnetic field. The time along the segments is flow time. Flow time is not physical time. A step of flow time is not a step of the fluid.

## g09.cond

duration: 0:35
badge: PROVED

### cue.g09.cond.show

role: spoken

The same network receives the parameter. The parameter enters beside the noise, so one network serves the whole sweep. A filled tick is a value the training saw. An open tick is a test. The network has to answer on an open tick without having stored that value. Open ticks here are interpolation. They do not step past the trained range. That is the same kind of test you meet again when a later bench is bounded.

## g09.why

duration: 0:40
badge: PROVED

### cue.g09.why.show

role: spoken

The path is straight so a stationarity loss can differentiate through the flow. If the loss cares about a stationary cloud, its gradient has to travel back along the path that built the sample. A straight path keeps that trip simple. The scheme that integrates the flow is called Heun. Heun is the stepper. Diffusion stays open. The offer left both samplers available, and these runs picked the straight path. Flow time is still not physical time. It is the coordinate of the walk, not the time in the fluid equations.

## g09.sizes

duration: 0:25
badge: MEASURED

### cue.g09.sizes.show

role: spoken

Variants A, B, and C of E1 use an MLP of width 256 and 12,000 iterations. E2 uses a width of 1024 and 6,000 iterations, taken in 50 Heun steps. Width means the hidden layer. Iterations are training steps, not steps of the fluid. The same stepper walks the flow on both benches.
