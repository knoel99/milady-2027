**Figure — The internship's three test benches simulate systems of increasing complexity, at parameter values placed on both sides of their transitions.** **E1**, Gissinger's (2012) three-mode reversal model, integrated with RK4 ($\Delta t = 0.01$, transient 300, 16 trajectories of length 3,000 per value of $\Gamma$); the curve gives the frequency of zero crossings of the dipole $D$ in the fine-grid simulations (up to 0.011 per time unit): zero up to $\Gamma = 0.86$, positive from 0.865. **E2**, forced 2D MHD in potentials ($32^2$, 2/3 dealiasing, white forcing in the shell $2 \le |\mathbf{k}| \le 3$, 16 members per value of $\eta$); the curve gives the mean magnetic energy $E_B$ (from 0.30 to 0.13, band: standard deviation across snapshots), **recomputed** for this figure with the same solver and settings, since E2's raw data are not versioned. **E3**, kinematic dynamo of the ABC flow ($32^3$, pseudo-spectral, integrating-factor RK4); the curve gives the growth rate $\lambda(Rm)$, positive in the windows $8.87 < Rm < 17.6$ and $Rm > 26.8$ (shaded); the axis is restricted to $\pm 0.012$, and the three points below the scale ($\lambda = -0.50$, $-0.15$ and $-0.046$ at $Rm = 2$, 4 and 6) are marked with an arrow. Filled points: training values; open points: test values (E1, E2). E2 and E3 have their campaign in the infrastructure (`palier1_mhd2d`, same solver; `palier2_abc3d`, same flow solved by the 3D solver at $64^3$); E1 remains a standalone script.
