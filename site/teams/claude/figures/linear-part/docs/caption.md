**Why the first generators are over-dispersed, and what was tried.** (a) The optimal flow-matching velocity field splits into a linear part and the rest; for Gaussian data it is exactly affine. The latent has 5320 dimensions for the potentials models (W4, W4s) and 7980 for the free components (W0); the network has width 512 (neck drawn to scale for 5320). The statement that a network narrower than the latent cannot carry the linear part is a conjecture, tested by a width scan (hypothesis H21). Measured: the flow-matching loss of the plain models W0, W4, W4s after training is 1.60-1.63, above pi/2 = 1.571, the loss of the exact linear field for independent unit Gaussian coordinates; the potentials models have 2 to 3 times too many null points. (b) Six models on the same glyphs: W4s (same network, no linear part), W10 (exact Gaussian field plus network), W9 (learned affine term plus network), W11 (exact Gaussian field, learned affine correction and network) G0 (Gaussian law per Fourier mode, no covariance between modes, no network) and G1 (Gaussian law with the covariance between modes, no network; darker green). The two Gaussian laws without a network are closer to the reference than every learned model, and G1 is the best on all three columns (energy distance over floor 2.6, null-count error 1.10, restart drift 0.06). Markers show seed means over 5 seeds of the energy distance to the reference over the noise floor, on the held-out magnetic diffusivity (log axis); the table gives the null-count error and the restart drift in relative values.
